# Relative Strength Index (RSI) — Investopedia (fragmentos vía búsqueda)

## What is the Relative Strength Index?

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum indicator that measures the magnitude of recent price changes to analyze overbought or oversold conditions. It charts the current and historical strength or weakness of a stock or market based on the closing prices of a recent trading period.

## Key Characteristics

The RSI is displayed as an oscillator (a line graph that moves between two extremes) and can have a reading from 0 to 100. The RSI is most typically used on a 14-day timeframe, with high and low levels marked at 70 and 30, respectively.

## Overbought and Oversold Conditions

Traditional interpretation and usage of the RSI is that values of 70 or above indicate that a security is becoming overbought or overvalued and may be primed for a trend reversal or corrective pullback in price.

## Historical Background

The indicator was originally developed by J. Welles Wilder Jr. and introduced in his seminal 1978 book, *New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems*.

## Usage Considerations

As long as an asset's price momentum remains strong (either up or down) the indicator can stay in overbought or oversold territory for long periods of time. Therefore, the RSI is most trustworthy in an oscillating market when the price is alternating between bullish and bearish periods.

## Formula (según fragmentos de búsqueda, no verificado verbatim contra el original)

```
RSI = 100 - [100 / (1 + (Average of Upward Price Change / Average of Downward Price Change))]
```
