# Introduction to Candlesticks (StockCharts ChartSchool)

## History of Candlestick Charts

The Japanese employed technical analysis for rice trading during the 17th century. While their methods differed from Charles Dow's American approach around 1900, they shared common principles: price action matters more than news, all information reflects in prices, and markets move through buyer/seller emotions and expectations.

According to Steve Nison, candlestick charting appeared after 1850, with credit largely going to Homma, a legendary rice trader from Sakata. His original concepts underwent refinement over many trading years, eventually becoming the modern candlestick system.

## Formation of Candlestick Charts

Creating candlesticks requires datasets containing "open, high, low and close values for each time period." The hollow or filled portion represents "the body" (or "real body"), while upper and lower thin lines called "shadows" (or "wicks" and "tails") show the high/low range.

Hollow candlesticks form when closing prices exceed opening prices, with the body bottom showing the open and top showing the close. Filled candlesticks appear when closing prices fall below opening prices, with reversed body positioning.

Traders prefer candlesticks over traditional bar charts for visual appeal and interpretability. "Hollow candlesticks, where the close is greater than the open, indicate buying pressure. Filled candlesticks, where the close is less than the open, indicate selling pressure."

## Long Versus Short Bodies

Body length indicates intensity of buying or selling pressure. "Generally speaking, the longer the body is, the more intense the buying or selling pressure. Conversely, short candlesticks indicate little price movement."

Long white candlesticks demonstrate strong buying pressure, with the close significantly above the open. After extended declines, they may mark turning points or support levels.

Long black candlesticks show strong selling pressure, with closes well below opens. After advances, they can signal upcoming reversals or resistance levels.

Marubozu candlesticks represent potent variations without upper or lower shadows. "A white Marubozu forms when the open equals the low and the close equals the high," indicating buyers controlled action throughout. Black Marubozu form oppositely, showing seller dominance.

## Long Versus Short Shadows

Shadows reveal trading session information. Upper shadows represent session highs, lower shadows represent lows. "Candlesticks with short shadows indicate that most of the trading action was confined near the open and close."

Long upper shadows with short lower shadows suggest buyers initially dominated, bidding prices higher, but sellers ultimately forced prices down. Conversely, long lower shadows with short upper shadows indicate sellers drove prices lower, but buyers resurfaced by session end.

Spinning tops feature long upper shadows, long lower shadows, and small real bodies. They "represent indecision. The small real body (whether hollow or filled) shows little movement from open to close." After advances or declines, spinning tops signal potential trend weakness or interruption.

## Doji

Doji form when security open and close prices are virtually equal. "The length of the upper and lower shadows can vary, with the resulting candlestick looking like a cross, inverted cross, or plus sign. Alone, doji are neutral patterns."

Doji convey "a sense of indecision or tug-of-war between buyers and sellers." Neither group gained control, potentially signaling a developing turning point. Different securities require different robustness criteria; a $20 stock might have 0.125-point open/close difference, while a $200 stock might need 1.25 points.

### Doji and Trend

Doji relevance depends on preceding trends. After advances or long white candlesticks, doji signal weakening buying pressure. After declines or long black candlesticks, they indicate diminishing selling pressure.

"Doji indicate that the forces of supply and demand are becoming more evenly matched and a change in trend may be near." However, they require further confirmation before indicating reversals.

After long white candlesticks, doji suggest uptrends may be ending, needing confirmation through gap downs, long black candlesticks, or declines below the white candlestick's open.

After long black candlesticks, doji indicate downtrends may be ending, requiring confirmation through gap ups, long white candlesticks, or advances above the black candlestick's open.

### Long-Legged Doji

Long-legged doji have "long upper and lower shadows almost equal in length. They reflect the market's indecision." These candlesticks show prices trading well above and below opening levels but closing virtually even with the open, resulting in minimal change despite significant intraday movement.

### Dragonfly and Gravestone Doji

**Dragonfly Doji:** Form when open, high, and close are equal, with a long lower shadow creating a "T" shape. "Dragonfly doji indicate that sellers dominated trading and drove prices lower during the session. By the end of the session, buyers resurfaced." Reversal implications depend on prior price action and confirmation. After downtrends, long black candlesticks, or at support levels, dragonfly doji could signal potential bullish reversals. After uptrends, long white candlesticks, or at resistance, they might foreshadow bearish reversals.

**Gravestone Doji:** Form when open, low, and close are equal, with a long upper shadow creating an inverted "T" shape. "Gravestone doji indicate buyers dominated trading and drove prices higher during the session. However, by the end of the session, sellers resurfaced." Like dragonfly doji, reversal implications depend on context and confirmation.

## Bulls Versus Bears

Candlesticks depict battles between buyers and sellers over specific periods. Six basic candlestick types emerge:

1. Long white candlesticks indicate bulls controlled most trading
2. Long black candlesticks indicate bears controlled most trading
3. Small candlesticks show neither side moved prices significantly
4. Long lower shadows indicate bears controlled early action but bulls staged comebacks
5. Long upper shadows indicate bulls controlled early action but bears staged comebacks
6. Long upper and lower shadows indicate both sides had moments with resulting standoffs

## What Candlesticks Don't Tell You

Candlesticks don't reveal sequences of events between open and close—only open/close relationships. "Candlesticks still offer valuable information on the relative positions of the open, high, low, and close. However, the trading activity that forms a particular candlestick can vary." Multiple high/low sequences can create identical candlesticks. Two moves might portray different market dynamics than three moves, yet appear identical on charts.

## Prior Trend

Per Greg Morris in *Candlestick Charting Explained*, reversal patterns require prior trends to reverse. "Bullish reversals require a preceding downtrend, and bearish reversals require a prior uptrend." Trend direction can be determined using trend lines, moving averages, or peak/trough analysis. Because candlesticks are short-term, "it is usually best to consider the last 1-4 weeks of price action."

## Candlestick Positioning

### Star Position

Candlesticks gapping away from previous candlesticks occupy "star position." First candlesticks typically have large real bodies, while second candlesticks have small real bodies. Doji, hammers, shooting stars, and spinning tops with small bodies can form in this position.

### Harami Position

Candlesticks forming within previous candlesticks' real bodies occupy "Harami position." Harami means pregnant in Japanese; "appropriately, the second candlestick is nestled inside the first." First candlesticks typically have large real bodies, seconds have smaller bodies.

## Long Shadow Reversals

Two pairs of single candlestick reversal patterns exist: small real bodies with one long shadow and one short or non-existent shadow. Long shadows should be "at least twice the length of the real body."

Hammer and Hanging Man consist of identical candlesticks with small bodies and long lower shadows. Shooting Star and Inverted Hammer contain identical candlesticks with small bodies and long upper shadows. Prior price action determines bullish or bearish classification.

### Hammer and Hanging Man

Both feature "small real bodies (black or white), long lower shadows, and short or non-existent upper shadows."

**Hammer:** A bullish reversal pattern forming after declines. "After a decline, hammers signal a bullish revival. The low of the long lower shadow implies that sellers drove prices lower during the session. However, the strong finish indicates that buyers regained their footing." Hammers require bullish confirmation before action, as the low indicates remaining sellers. Confirmation might come through gap ups or long white candlesticks on expanding volume.

**Hanging Man:** A bearish reversal pattern forming after advances. "Forming after an advance, a Hanging Man signals that selling pressure is starting to increase. The low of the long lower shadow confirms that sellers pushed prices lower during the session." Hanging Man candlesticks require bearish confirmation, potentially through gap downs or long black candlesticks on heavy volume.

### Inverted Hammer and Shooting Star

Both feature "small real bodies (black or white), long upper shadows and small or nonexistent lower shadows."

**Shooting Star:** A bearish reversal pattern forming after advances and in star position. The candlestick forms "when prices gap higher on the open, advance during the session, and close well off their highs." The long upper shadow indicates bears forced prices down after large advances. "To indicate a substantial reversal, the upper shadow should be relatively long and at least 2 times the length of the body." Bearish confirmation through gap downs or long black candlesticks on heavy volume is required.

**Inverted Hammer:** Forms after declines or downtrends, representing potential reversals or support levels. "After a decline, the long upper shadow indicates buying pressure during the session. However, the bulls were not able to sustain this buying pressure and prices closed well off of their highs." Bullish confirmation is required before action.

## Blending Candlesticks

Candlestick patterns can blend into single candlesticks using: "The open of the first candlestick, the close of the last candlestick, [and] the high and low of the pattern." Bullish Engulfing or Piercing patterns blend into Hammers, while Bearish Engulfing or Dark Cloud Cover patterns blend into Shooting Stars. Three White Soldiers blend into long white candlesticks; Three Black Crows blend into long black candlesticks.

# Candlesticks and Traditional Chart Analysis (StockCharts ChartSchool, complementaria)

## Benefits of Candlestick Charts

Candlestick charts have merits as a charting system, but they also confirm signals generated by other technical analysis tools — moving averages, breakout signals, head and shoulders patterns, and volume — forming mutually beneficial relationships that strengthen investor confidence when analyzing charts.

## Candlesticks and Moving Averages

A key feature provided by candlestick patterns is the ability to confirm moving average signals. Example: two separate candlestick patterns—*spinning top* and *doji*—followed by a long white (hollow) candlestick added bullish confirmation of a 200-day moving average acting as support. The same patterns confirmed the 30 level on RSI as an oversold condition.

## Candlesticks and Breakouts

Candlesticks can confirm breakouts from traditional chart patterns found within congestion zones. When a bullish or bearish candlestick pattern occurs within the vicinity of a traditional breakout, it adds validity to the direction of that breakout. Example: the top line of a triangle touched twice by *spinning-top* candlesticks (indecision), then a *bearish harami* pattern followed by another down day, then a gap down with a long-filled candlestick — all adding up to an overall bearish picture despite an ascending triangle traditionally being a bullish pattern. An equivalent OHLC bar chart on the same data contains less useful information for anticipating the breakout direction.

## Candlesticks and the Head & Shoulders Pattern

One limitation of candlestick patterns alone is they don't provide potential price targets — this can be achieved by combining candlesticks with other technical analysis techniques, such as the traditional head & shoulders price target calculation (distance from the top of the head to the neckline, subtracted from the neckline breakout point).

## Candlesticks and Volume

Volume can be used to confirm candlestick patterns — e.g. volume increasing during a *bearish harami* formation, spiking at a breakout, and a *doji* formed on huge volume preceding a continued sell-off.

## Concluding Thoughts

Candlestick patterns can be helpful in identifying potential changes in market direction. When used with traditional technical analysis, they add confirmation to those signals. The more supporting/confirming information you can add to your analysis of chart patterns, the more conviction you will have in your trading decisions — and conflicting signals give you the opportunity to decide whether to skip a trade altogether.
