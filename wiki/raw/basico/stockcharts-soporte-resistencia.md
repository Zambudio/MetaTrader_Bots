# Support & Resistance (StockCharts ChartSchool)

## Core Definitions

**Support** is defined as "the price level at which demand is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from declining further." As prices fall toward support, buyers become more willing to purchase while sellers hesitate.

**Resistance** represents "the price level at which selling is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from rising further." As prices climb toward resistance, sellers become more eager to sell while buyers pull back.

## Why These Levels Function

Supply and demand dynamics drive price movements. When demand exceeds supply, prices rise; when supply exceeds demand, prices fall. Support and resistance levels represent equilibrium points where these forces temporarily balance. Neither level is absolute—prices can breach them when market sentiment shifts decisively.

## Identification Methods

**Previous Highs and Lows**: Support emerges from prior reaction lows, while resistance forms at previous reaction highs. These levels gain strength as prices test them repeatedly without breaking through.

**Role Reversal**: A broken support level can transform into resistance, and vice versa. When a support level breaks downward, previously bullish buyers holding losing positions create an "overhead supply overhang" that becomes resistance.

The NASDAQ 100 example demonstrates this: after breaking resistance at 935 in May 1997, that level became a new support zone when the index subsequently declined to test it.

## Trading Ranges

When prices move sideways within tight boundaries, supply and demand are balanced. A breakout signals victory for either bulls (upside break) or bears (downside break).

## Support & Resistance Zones

For volatile, extended trading ranges spanning months, traders often establish zones rather than precise levels, accounting for volatility and the non-exact nature of technical analysis.
