# Chart Types: Line, Bar, and OHLC Charts (StockCharts ChartSchool — "What Are Charts?")

Página que cubre los tres tipos base de gráfico de precio. Complementa el índice de tipos especializados ya capturado en `stockcharts-tipos-grafico.md` (que explícitamente señalaba como hueco la ausencia de páginas dedicadas a línea/barras/velas básicas). Esta página sí las cubre.

## Line Charts

Line charts focus exclusively on closing prices, disregarding intraday fluctuations. According to the documentation, "Some investors and traders consider the closing level to be more important than the open, high, or low."

**Construction:** A line connects closing prices across periods, creating a simplified visualization.

**Advantages:**
- Reduces visual clutter by ignoring intraday swings
- Useful when only closing data is available (certain indices, thinly-traded stocks)

**Limitations:** Lacks detail on price ranges within each period.

## Bar Charts

Bar charts display high, low, and close prices for each period using vertical bars with horizontal tick marks.

**Construction:** The vertical bar represents the high and low; a small horizontal line marks the close. Optional open data appears as a left-extending line.

**Advantages:**
- Displays substantial data efficiently
- Relatively compact format allows fitting many bars while maintaining clarity
- Effective for analyzing close relative to high-low ranges

**Limitations:** Including open data can create clutter; candlestick charts may offer better alternatives when open prices matter.

## Candlestick Charts

Originating in Japan over three centuries ago, candlesticks require open, high, low, and close data.

**Construction:** The body (rectangular area) represents open-to-close range; shadows (lines extending above/below) show high and low. "Hollow candlesticks form when the close is higher than the open, and filled candlesticks form when the close is lower."

**Advantages:** Traders appreciate their readability, particularly the open-close relationship clarity.

**Enhancement:** Color coding (green for higher closes, red for lower) aids visual interpretation.

## Nota de contexto adicional (de la búsqueda que localizó esta fuente)

De la página relacionada "Candlesticks and Traditional Chart Analysis" (mismo ChartSchool): los line charts se usan cuando no hay datos de open/high/low disponibles — típico en ciertos índices, valores poco negociados, y precios intradía en tiempo real de algunos proveedores. Comparados con velas, los gráficos de barras OHLC transmiten la misma información pero muchos traders los consideran menos legibles visualmente que las velas.
