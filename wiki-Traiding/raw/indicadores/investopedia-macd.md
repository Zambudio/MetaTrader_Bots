# MACD — fragmentos genéricos vía búsqueda (no confirmado como texto de Investopedia)

## Definition

MACD is a momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two Exponential Moving Averages (EMAs) of a stock price. It's a technical indicator designed to help investors identify price trends, measure trend momentum, and identify acceleration points to fine-tune market entry timing.

## How It Works

It is calculated by subtracting the 26-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the 12-period EMA, creating the MACD line. A 9-period EMA of the MACD line, known as the signal line, is then plotted to generate buy or sell signals.

Convergence means that two moving averages are coming together, while divergence means that they're moving away from each other.

## Key Components

The MACD consists of three parts: the MACD line (the difference between the 12-period and 26-period EMA), the signal line (often a 9-period EMA of the MACD line), and the histogram (which shows the difference between the MACD line and the signal line).

## History

It was developed in the late 1970s by technical analyst Gerald Appel.
