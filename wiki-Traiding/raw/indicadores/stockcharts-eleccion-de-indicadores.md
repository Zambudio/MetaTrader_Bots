# Technical Indicators and Oscillators: Selection and Application (StockCharts ChartSchool)

## Choosing Indicators Carefully

The documentation emphasizes a measured approach to indicator selection. Rather than attempting to monitor numerous indicators, traders should choose an indicator to use for analysis "carefully and moderately." Attempts to cover more than five indicators are usually futile; it is best to focus on two or three indicators and learn their intricacies inside and out.

## Complementary Selection Strategy (avoiding redundancy)

When building an indicator toolkit, avoid redundancy: choose indicators that complement each other, instead of those that move in unison and generate the same signals. Explicit example given: it would be redundant to use two indicators that are both good for showing overbought and oversold levels, such as the Stochastic Oscillator and RSI — both measure momentum and both have overbought/oversold levels, so combining them adds little beyond what either gives alone.

## Indicator Categories

Two main categories:

- **Leading indicators**: generate early signals with higher frequency — Commodity Channel Index, Momentum, RSI, Stochastic Oscillator, Williams %R. Work best in trading (range-bound) markets but carry greater risk of false signals.
- **Lagging/trend-following indicators**: follow price action — moving averages, MACD. Work best during sustained trends but lag (signal late) during sideways/choppy markets.

Within oscillators specifically, two further sub-types:

- **Centered oscillators** (MACD, ROC — Rate of Change): fluctuate around a zero line, identifying the strength/weakness or direction of momentum.
- **Banded oscillators** (RSI, Stochastics): move within upper/lower bounds, identifying overbought and oversold extremes.

## Key Application Principles

"The trend is your friend, and it can be dangerous to fight it" — in an uptrend, favor oversold buy signals; in a downtrend, favor overbought sell signals (i.e. trade with the dominant trend rather than purely counter-trend on oscillator extremes).

Combine multiple *confirming* signals from different sources before acting — the example given: a buy signal might be generated with an oversold reading, a positive (bullish) divergence, and a bullish moving-average crossover together, rather than trading any single one of those in isolation.
